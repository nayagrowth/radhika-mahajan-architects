# Designing for Light and Climate: Lessons from Residential Architecture in Pune & Lonavala

Maharashtra's Deccan plateau experiences distinct micro-climates—from Pune's warm summer afternoons and pleasant evenings to Lonavala's heavy monsoon downpours and cool winters.

Architecture and interior design cannot be detached from these geographic realities. Designing without climate awareness leads to overheated living rooms, excessive air conditioning electricity bills, and poor indoor air circulation.

---

## Passive Design Strategies We Implement

### 1. Sun-Path & Glare Management
- **West-Facing Facades**: We employ vertical louvers, deep balcony overhangs, and dual-layer sheer drapery to block harsh 4 PM afternoon heat while welcoming diffused daylight.
- **North & East Openings**: Maximizing floor-to-ceiling glass to draw soft, glare-free illumination into master bedrooms and study areas.

### 2. Cross-Ventilation Corridors
By positioning opposite openings in the living-dining continuum, air naturally flows through the residence, reducing indoor temperatures by 3°C to 5°C without turning on an AC.

### 3. Indoor-Outdoor Terrace Blur in Lonavala Villas
In Lonavala retreats, we design deep covered verandas with locally quarried basalt stone that act as thermal buffers while allowing families to enjoy monsoon rains without water ingress.
